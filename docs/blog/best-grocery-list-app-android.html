Best grocery list apps for Android in 2026

ShelfTrack · August 2026 · 6 min read

A good grocery list app does more than let you type names into a list. The best ones work offline, sync with your household, scan barcodes, and help you avoid buying things you already have at home. Here is what to look for — and how the main options compare.

What makes a good grocery list app?

Before comparing apps, here are the features that actually matter in daily use:

  • Works offline — your list should be readable in a supermarket without signal
  • Barcode scanning — add products by scan instead of typing
  • Shared lists — sync with a partner or family member in real time
  • Expiry and pantry tracking — know what you already have and what is going off
  • Privacy — your grocery data should not be sold to advertisers
  • No forced account — core features should not require registration

OurGroceries — best for simple synced lists

OurGroceries is a long-established app focused purely on shared shopping lists. It syncs quickly across devices and supports multiple lists and categories. The free version shows ads; a one-time paid upgrade removes them. It does not track expiry dates or scan receipts.

Google Keep — best for basic lists with no setup

Google Keep is not a grocery app, but many people use it for shopping because it is already installed on most Android phones. It syncs across devices and supports shared notes. It has no barcode scan, no categories, no expiry tracking, and no receipt import. Good enough for occasional shoppers; outgrown quickly by regular ones.

Out of Milk — budget and pantry focused

Out of Milk combines a shopping list with a pantry list and a "to-do" list. It supports barcode scanning and syncs across devices. The UI is dated and some features require a premium subscription. Expiry tracking is basic.

AnyList — popular on iOS, limited on Android

AnyList is well-regarded among iPhone users for meal planning and recipe import. The Android version exists but feels secondary and lacks some features. If your household is mixed iOS/Android, this can be frustrating.
