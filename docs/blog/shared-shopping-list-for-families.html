Shared lists

How to share a grocery list with your partner or family

ShelfTrack · August 2026 · 4 min read

Two people shopping separately for the same house — with no shared list — almost always ends in duplicates, missed items, or a disagreement in the dairy aisle. A live shared grocery list that updates on both phones instantly solves all three.

The problem with texting grocery lists

Most couples and families share lists over WhatsApp or iMessage. This works until someone adds something after the other person has already left the house, or until the thread is buried under messages about something else entirely.

A dedicated shared grocery list keeps shopping separate from chat, updates in real time, and lets you check items off as you pick them up so your partner knows the progress without texting back and forth.

How live shared lists work in ShelfTrack

ShelfTrack uses a six-digit join code to connect two or more phones to the same list. There is no account to create — the join code is all you need.

  1. One person creates the list in ShelfTrack and taps the Share icon on the Lists screen.
  2. Enable live sharing — the app generates a six-digit code and uploads the list.
  3. The other person opens ShelfTrack, taps Join list, and enters the code.
  4. The list appears on both phones. Any change on either phone — adding an item, checking one off, changing the quantity, or deleting — syncs to the other within a few seconds.

The list owner can stop sharing at any time. Other members can leave from their own device.

Use cases — when a shared grocery list really helps

Weekly family shop

Build the list together during the week: one person adds what they notice running low in the kitchen, the other adds things for meals they are planning. By shopping day the list is already complete with no last-minute discussion.

Splitting the shop

One partner takes the fresh produce section while the other does dry goods. Both can check off items independently and see what the other has already picked up in real time. No doubling back across the store.

Someone shops on the way home

The person at home adds milk and bread to the list while the other is already near the shops. The addition appears immediately — no call needed.

Housemates with shared basics

A shared list for kitchen staples that anyone in the house can add to means no one has to coordinate who is buying washing-up liquid this week.

Tips for using a shared grocery list well

  • Use one list per shop — a "weekly shop" list stays cleaner than a single running list that never gets cleared
  • Check items off as you put them in the basket, not when you get home — your partner can see progress in real time
  • Add quantities — "milk x2" prevents guessing at the shelf
  • Voice add when your hands are full — ShelfTrack has a mic button on the list screen so you can add items while cooking without touching your phone

Privacy and data

ShelfTrack shared lists use anonymous sign-in behind the scenes — no email, no password. The list content is stored on Supabase servers only while the list is live-shared. When you stop sharing, the connection is removed. Your private lists stay entirely on your device.
